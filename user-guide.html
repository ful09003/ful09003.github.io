User Guide

Once upon a time, I worked alongside an individual who introduced me to the idea of a "personal user guide".

The goal of a "personal user guide" as I understood it, is to convey preferences on how, when, and where communications happen, as well as other 'tips/tricks/wtfs' when working with me professionally.

I don't know if it's a good idea; these things change over time and I'm not sure I'd want to work somewhere that the culture necessitates these guides as a first principle (just treat everyone with empathy and kindness, but I digress..) - but I do think it's a neat idea, and so I shall endeavor to try it out. While these are written in the form of what _I_ do/think/feel, they should also highlight a team or company culture I'd do well in. If any of these are _not_ happening today where you are, let's chat about why not!

Tenets and Values I Hold (more or less in order of how ingrained they are)

  • People are not abstractions. Care for them as much as possible.
  • (Making Shrek proud) Failure is like an onion - the more layers peeled back, the more systemic issues leading to that failure are discovered. Always try seeking those issues, as they are the foundation for a better future.
  • Most of us aren't saving or putting lives at risk. It's often (not always) not that serious.
  • Try, even if it's all I can do
  • Capitalism is a self-defeating system that we all must live with
  • Choose whimsy
  • Choose cooperation
  • Choose to forgive
  • Work in the open

Communication (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • Unless it's to share popcorn, memes, or discuss truly sensitive topics, I prefer to have little-to-no-DMs
  • Work in the open
  • I REPEAT: WORK IN THE OPEN
  • I probably will read all 40 pages of an overarchitected RFC. It will be slow. The pain we both experience as a result is proportional to page count.
  • When I ask questions, it's because I truly don't understand a topic, or have overthought something and need some clarity on intent or meaning.
  • I have a tendency to think out loud, including counterarguments to a given matter. I don't savor doing that. Please call me out on it if you don't like it, either!
  • I will probably read Slack messages after-hours. I probably will not reply after-hours if non-urgent. I will probably spend all night/weekend/vacation stressing about the message, though.
  • I will do everything in my power to ensure my communication uplifts others. The feelings we feel and psychological safety to feel them _are_ serious, and what matter most in our communication.
  • I tend to type or speak too much. It's gotten better, but please call me out on it if I ramble.

Disagreements and Feedback (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • The amount I dig my heels in tends to be proportional to how strongly I feel a given direction damages others. Damage in this case often (not always) meaning emotional or psychological, and applied to colleagues, stakeholders, then users (generally in that order).
  • I tend to not hold my opinions too tightly. I don't like the ego of a phrase like 'disagree but commit'. It should be more like: 'Trust, but share concerns'
  • I tend to have visceral disagreements to interactions starting with tossing issues over a fence and/or making bold, unsubstantiated claims. It's better to drive to agreement of the problem first, align on options to reduce the harm it causes, then pick and work towards a solution.
  • Offering positive feedback in public is great! Offering less-positive feedback is better in private (maybe the one valid use of a DM), unless the situation feels entirely out-of-hand and needing immediate intercession.
  • I try to avoid interjecting my opinion when unsolicited. It's hard to balance that with wanting to see others succeed. If I can lift with someone on a matter they're struggling with, I'll tend towards doing that. If not and all I can offer is just listening, I absolutely will just listen.

Meetings (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • Please have at least a bare-bones agenda available
  • I'll always have some amount of focus/personal/whatever time blocked out on my calendar. Please try to honor it, but know I won't be mad if we need to meet during it.
  • It's totally fine if we don't use an entire meeting block
  • I try to always set up recurring meetings with my peers. It is our time to lift and level each other up, stay connected, have a safe space outside of a management chain, and build the team culture. These are never "need to attend" things, and does not replace a good manager. I feel it is a net positive for building a team culture, though.

Code Reviews/Writing (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • I don't have strong preference about (un-)conventional commit messages
  • I care that a pull request, at least, has context about /why/ a change is happening. Time erodes that context, preserving it while freshest pays repeatedly.
  • I tend to mix nits, thinking-out-louds, and actionable feedback. I'll do my best to signify which is which.
  • If a review is needed urgently, I'll make that clear. Most things aren't that urgent.

Miscellaneous (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • I really care about docs. Please have only one place for docs, or give me the time and freedom to at least clean up "our own" docs and file them to a single place.
  • We're all human - take time off. Encourage others to.
  • "Run this script once a day/week/month" is not scary to me. Making the results understandable and repeatable is the challenge. The curious drive to endlessly automate is a mystery to me.
