User Guide

Once upon a time, I worked alongside an individual who introduced me to the idea of a "personal user guide".

The goal of a "personal user guide" as I understood it, is to convey preferences on how, when, and where communications happen, as well as other 'tips/tricks/wtfs' when working with me professionally.

I don't know if it's a good idea; these things change over time and I'm not sure I'd want to work somewhere that the culture necessitates it (just treat everyone with empathy and kindness, but I digress..) - but I do think it's a neat idea, and so I shall endeavor to describe these things. While these are written in the form of what _I_ do/think/feel, they should also highlight a team culture I'd do well in. If any of these are _not_ a team culture, let's chat about why not!

Tenets and Values I Hold (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • Failures /should/ be formative for something better
  • Most of us aren't saving or putting lives at risk. It's often not that serious
  • (Making Shrek proud), Failure is like an onion - the more layers peeled back, the more we both discover systemic issues at fault. Always try seeking those issues.
  • Try, even if it's all I can do
  • Capitalism is a self-defeating system that we all must live with
  • Choose whimsy
  • Choose cooperation
  • Forgive
  • Work in the open
  • "Run this script once a day/week/month" is not scary to me. Making the results understandable and repeatable is the challenge. The curious drive to endlessly automate is a mystery to me.

Communication (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • Unless it's to share popcorn, memes, or discuss truly sensitive topics, I prefer to have little-to-no-DMs
  • Work in the open
  • I REPEAT: WORK IN THE OPEN
  • I probably will read all 40 pages of an overarchitected RFC. It will be slow. The pain we both experience as a result is proportional to page count. If I ask questions, it's because I truly don't understand.
  • I will probably read Slack messages after-hours. I probably will not reply that night. I will probably spend all night/weekend/vacation stressing about the message.
  • I will do everything in my power to ensure my communication uplifts others. Again - failure should be formative, is probably a result of systemic issue, and not that serious. The feelings felt and psychological safety to feel them _are_ serious though, and what matter most in our communication.
  • I tend to type or speak too much. It's gotten better, but please call me out on it if I ramble.

Meetings (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • Please have at least a bare-bones agenda available
  • I'll always have some amount of focus/personal/whatever time. Please try to honor it, but know I won't be mad if we need to meet during it.
  • It's totally fine if we don't use the entire time
  • I will set up recurring meetings with my peers. It is our time to lift and level each other up, stay connected, have a safe space outside of a management chain, and build the team culture. These are never a "need to attend", and does not replace a good manager.

Code Reviews/Writing (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • I don't have strong preference about (un-)conventional commit messages
  • I care that a pull request, at least, has context about /why/ a change is happening. Time erodes that context, preserving it while freshest pays repeatedly.
  • I tend to mix nits, thinking-out-louds, and actionable feedback. I'll do my best to signify which is which.
  • If a review is needed urgently, I'll make that clear. Most things aren't that urgent.

Miscellaneous (no order, just brain-dumping)

  • I really care about docs. Please have only one place for docs, or give me the time and range to at least clean up "our own" docs and file to a single place.
  • We're all human - take time off. Encourage others to.
